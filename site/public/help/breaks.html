Screen breaks

Hotsauce can remind you to look away from the screen now and then: every 20 minutes for 20 seconds to start (the 20-20-20 rule for tired eyes), or whatever timing suits you. It is off until you turn it on.

Turn it on

  1. On a computer, click your initials (or your photo) at the bottom of the left bar, then Breaks. It turns on at once, and the row says how often.
  2. To change how often, how long, the animation or the chime, open Settings. Breaks is on the first page, General.

New to Hotsauce? The Getting started tile on Today asks once whether you’d like screen breaks. Press Turn on or No thanks.

Your settings are yours, not the device’s: they follow you to every browser and the Mac app.

What a break looks like

Ten seconds before a break, a small note says Screen break in 10s, with Start now, 5 more minutes and Skip. Then the screen dims and a calm animation plays with a countdown: a breathing circle, drifting clouds or slow waves, whichever you picked. Look at something far away until it ends. A soft chime says the break is over; you can turn it off.

Skip and 5 more minutes are always there, and Esc skips. If your device is set to reduce motion, the animation stays still.

Settings

  • Every: from 5 minutes to 2 hours.
  • For: from 10 seconds to 15 minutes.
  • Animation: breathing circle, drifting clouds or slow waves, each with a preview.
  • Chime when the break ends: on to start.
  • Only during working hours: no breaks outside the hours and days you pick, in your own time zone.
  • Take a break now starts one straight away.

It waits when you’re busy

A break never interrupts a recording, dictation, screen share or a meeting on your calendar with other people. It comes about a minute after you finish instead. Time away from the computer counts as a break, so the count starts again when you come back.

In the Mac app

The Mac app shows breaks over every display, above all your apps, even when the Hotsauce window is closed. It also waits while you hold the Dictate key or the app records a call, and counts the time your Mac is locked or asleep as a break.

In a browser, breaks show in the Hotsauce tab, and only one tab shows them however many you have open. If the tab is in the background and you’ve allowed notifications, you get one when a break starts.

What it needs

Nothing. Breaks are off until each person turns them on.
