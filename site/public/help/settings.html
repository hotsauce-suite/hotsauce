Settings and notifications

Settings is where you set things up for yourself. On a computer, click your initials (or your photo) at the bottom of the left bar, then Settings. The same menu switches between light and dark, plays music, and opens the keyboard shortcuts and help. On a phone, tap Apps, then Settings.

Settings is a few small pages. Pick one from the menu on the left (on a phone, the row along the top). Help, the FAQ, Getting started (it brings back the first-day tile on Today), Send feedback (if your admin turned it on), What’s new and the version you’re running sit under the menu on every page. What’s new (or clicking the version) lists the changes in each release, in plain words; they’re also at hotsauce.work/changes.

Find a setting

Type in Search settings at the top of the menu (or press / on any Settings page). Everyday words work too: “dark mode” finds Appearance, “logo” finds Brand, “Claude” finds the AI tools. Pick a result and Settings opens that page at the right section, marked for a moment. Settings sections also show up in the command menu (⌘ K) once you start typing. Admins also find the Organization pages’ settings.

What’s in Settings

This Mac

Only in the Mac app, at the top of the menu. ⌘ , opens it. These settings stay on that Mac.

Dictation
The Dictation shortcut you hold to dictate, the Microphone for dictation (your Mac’s default, or any microphone it has; meeting recording keeps the default), Clean up or Literal, and Edge glow while dictating.
Meetings
Meeting reminders a minute before each meeting, and Offer to take notes when a call starts.
App
Open at login and Show in menu bar.
Permissions
Whether the microphone, Accessibility, Location and Notifications are allowed, an Allow… button for any that aren’t, and Run setup again.
Dictation history
How long your dictations are kept on this Mac (7, 30 or 90 days), and Clear history.
Dictate on this Mac
The account Dictate is connected as, and Disconnect this Mac.

General

Account
Your initials, name and email, and Sign out. Sign out lives only here.
Your photo
On General, under Account, press Add a photo and pick a picture of yourself (or click your face in the account menu). Hotsauce keeps a small square cut from its middle. Remove photo brings your initials back.
Notifications
The Notifications on this device switch covers everything Hotsauce sends: a reminder a minute before meetings, timed tasks when they’re due, check-in questions, and your daily briefing. Once it’s on, Send test notification sends a real one to every device you’ve switched on, so you can check it arrives.
Keyboard
Turn single-key shortcuts on or off, and see every shortcut.
Weather
Where Today’s weather is for: Use my location, Use a city, or Hide weather. With your location shared, it names the town or city you’re in (place names from OpenStreetMap). If your admin set a team city, Use the team’s city goes back to it.

Everything under On this device is remembered by the phone or computer you’re on, so each one can be set differently.

Appearance

Appearance
Light, dark, match your device, or a theme of your own. Under Make a theme, describe a look in your own words (“calm forest greens”, “a 1970s terminal”) and press Make theme: the whole app changes so you can try it. Describe a change (“darker”, “a warmer accent”) to adjust it, then Keep it or Discard it. A theme changes colours and fonts, and Hotsauce keeps its text readable. Your themes are yours alone and show up on every device you sign in on; Rename or Delete them under each one (up to 12). Making a theme needs the organization’s AI key. Below the themes, Ripple when switching tools (on) sends a liquid ripple from your click across the whole window when you open another tool, and Play a soft sound when switching tools (off) adds a quiet tick. Both are per device; the ripple is skipped when your device is set to reduce motion, and the sound stays silent while anything is recording.

Which look is on is remembered by each device. Your own themes are the exception to “this device only”: the list follows your account.

Connections

Calendars
Which of your Google calendars Meetings reads. Your main calendar is always on; tick any others.
Connect an AI tool
Make a personal token so an AI tool such as Claude, Cursor or Codex can work with your Hotsauce, with steps for each tool. See Connect an AI tool.

Apps

Mac app
The Mac app download. How to install it the first time opens the steps. Inside the Mac app, the card shows its version and says Up to date, or offers Restart to update once a newer one has downloaded (Download the update on an app that can’t update itself). In a browser it lists your Macs and the version each one has.
Web app
An Install button for this phone or computer; it shows Installed when you’re using the installed app. See Install it.
Chrome extension
The Chrome extension, for notes on a Google Meet call and saving pages to Links.

Admins also see an Organization group in the menu, for workspace-wide settings. See Run Hotsauce for your team.

Turn on notifications

  1. Open Settings. Notifications are on the first page, General.
  2. Turn on Notifications on this device and allow notifications when your browser asks.
  3. Press Send test notification. You should see it within a few seconds.

Each phone and computer is set up on its own. If Settings says notifications are blocked, allow them for Hotsauce in your browser’s site settings, then come back. If the test says it couldn’t reach a device, try again in a minute. On an iPhone, add Hotsauce to your home screen first. In the Mac app, Settings shows whether macOS allows Hotsauce’s notifications, and Open Notifications settings takes you to them (the first time, macOS asks).
